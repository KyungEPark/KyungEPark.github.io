On 12 August 2024 I presented at the 84th Academy of Management Annual Meeting, one of the largest conferences for management and organization scholars. The paper, “Stay for a Better Shot – Impact of Gender Quota on Female Entrepreneurship”, is co-authored with Baris Istipliler and examines how corporate gender quotas shape women’s individual employment decisions. It appears in the conference proceedings as Park, K. E., & Istipliler, B. (2024). Better Shot?: Impact of Gender Quota on Female Entrepreneurship, Evidence From Germany. *Academy of Management Proceedings*, 2024(1), 15671.

Using German data, we take a mobility perspective on entrepreneurship. Gender quotas are usually introduced to increase female representation in corporate settings, but they also change the advancement opportunities open to women, and with them the decision to stay in employment or leave it. Our findings suggest that quotas can shift those decisions substantially, by either improving or constraining career progression. This matters for policymakers, because it points to individual-level consequences that sit beneath the organizational and societal effects that are more commonly studied.

The study also looks at media perception, using large language models to analyse how gender quotas are discussed in news coverage. Tracking that coverage over time lets us ask whether media framing reinforces or weakens the changes in career advancement opportunities that the quotas bring about.
